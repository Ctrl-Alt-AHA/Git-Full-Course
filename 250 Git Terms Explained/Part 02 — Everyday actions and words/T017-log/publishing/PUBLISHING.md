## Patreon

Title: Reading Git history as a map

Imagine returning to a project and noticing a favorites button. You want to locate the recorded change connected to it. A history overview helps you narrow the search without opening every commit in detail.

Each entry offers signposts such as a commit identity, author, date and message. A graph adds parent relationships, making splits and joins easier to understand. Those edges describe ancestry. Their visual spacing does not measure elapsed time, and parallel lanes are not an execution schedule.

The default selected history begins at the current commit and follows its ancestors. A separate unmerged branch is not automatically included; merged work can be reachable through parent links.

Takeaway: find your place with the history map, then inspect a relevant commit. This lesson focuses on the overview; the companion Show lesson covers particular-commit detail.

Sources: https://git-scm.com/docs/git-log (DESCRIPTION, revision-range, --graph and PRETTY FORMATS).
